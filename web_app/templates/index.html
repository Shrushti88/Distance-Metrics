Similarity Measurement Using Distance Metrics

A Comprehensive Study & Benchmarking of Euclidean ($L_2$), Manhattan ($L_1$), and Cosine Distance Measures Across Low-to-High Dimensional Datasets.

Euclidean Distance ($L_2$)

Measures the true straight-line distance between points in Euclidean space.

Formula
$$d(u, v) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (u_i - v_i)^2} = \|u - v\|_2$$
  • Rotationally invariant
  • Satisfies all metric axioms
  • Sensitive to feature scale and high-dimensional noise

Manhattan Distance ($L_1$)

Measures grid-based distance along orthogonal coordinate axes (Taxicab metric).

Formula
$$d(u, v) = \sum_{i=1}^n |u_i - v_i| = \|u - v\|_1$$
  • Diamond-shaped iso-distance contours
  • Robust to extreme outlier dimensions
  • Maintains higher contrast in higher dimensions

Cosine Distance ($D_{cos}$)

Measures angular orientation difference between vectors, ignoring vector magnitude.

Formula
$$D_{cos} = 1 - \frac{u \cdot v}{\|u\|_2 \|v\|_2} = 1 - \cos(\theta)$$
  • Scale & magnitude invariant
  • Ideal for sparse, high-dimensional TF-IDF & embeddings
  • Bounded in $[0, 2]$ (or $[0, 1]$ for positive spaces)
